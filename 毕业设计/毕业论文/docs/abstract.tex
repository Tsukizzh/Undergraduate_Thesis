\begin{abstract}
  酶底物特异性预测是生物催化、合成生物学与药物研发中的核心任务。2025 年发表于 \textit{Nature} 的 EZSpecificity 模型是该任务的代表性先进方法，声称具有跨酶家族通用性；然而其训练数据集 ESIBank 在细胞色素 P450 超家族上覆盖稀缺（25{,}225 个酶中仅包含 389 个 P450，占 $1.5\%$）。鉴于 P450 在药物代谢、植物次生代谢与农药降解中的重要应用价值，可作为检验该通用性的严格外部评估基准。基于此，本研究开展三方面工作：其一，针对现有 P450 数据库多数仅提供酶序列、配体类型（底物/产物/抑制剂）记录不明的普遍局限，通过文献检索、PDB 结构分析与多轮人工审核，辅以多智能体协作流程，对整合自 RCSB PDB、ESIBank、P450Rdb、PlantP450DB 与 PCPD 五个来源的每条酶--配体记录严格复核与分类，构建了目前规模最大、标注最详细的 P450 酶--底物特异性公开基准数据集，含 1{,}622 个酶、2{,}125 个底物、47{,}510 条可用样本（正 4{,}362 / 负 43{,}148），显著扩展植物 P450 数据覆盖；其二，以 InterPro 功能域识别 ESIBank 原始训练集中的 389 个 P450 并过滤测试集对应样本，建立泄漏控制的外部公平对比协议；其三，引入残基级几何向量感知（GVP）通路构成双图架构，以零初始化加延迟解冻保证基线等价。在 7{,}963 条过滤后测试集（680 正 / 7{,}283 负）上，EZSpecificity 公开预训练模型 Test AUC 为 $0.5586$、AUPR 为 $0.1004$；而本研究领域专属基线 AUC 达到 $0.9205$、AUPR 为 $0.6403$，较 EZSpecificity 提高了 $0.3619$。双图架构在单随机切分下与基线相当，AUC 提高 $0.0008$、AUPR 下降 $0.0275$，未见显著收益。研究结果表明通用先进模型在训练集稀缺家族上存在明显的未见酶泛化瓶颈，领域专属数据重训可显著改善 P450 特异性预测表现。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract*}
  Enzyme--substrate specificity prediction is central to biocatalysis, synthetic biology, and drug discovery. EZSpecificity (\textit{Nature}, 2025) is a leading state-of-the-art model that claims cross-family generality. However, its training set ESIBank under-represents the cytochrome P450 superfamily (only 389 of 25{,}225 enzymes, $1.5\%$), while P450s play key roles in drug metabolism, plant secondary metabolism, and pesticide degradation---making them a rigorous external benchmark for that claim. First, addressing a key limitation of existing P450 databases---most provide only sequences or leave ligand types (substrate/product/inhibitor/cofactor) ambiguous---we integrated RCSB PDB, ESIBank, P450Rdb, PlantP450DB, and PCPD, and rigorously re-curated each enzyme--ligand record via literature retrieval, PDB structural analysis, and multi-round human review (assisted by a multi-agent pipeline). The resulting benchmark, to our knowledge the largest and most detailed public P450 enzyme--substrate specificity dataset, comprises $1{,}622$ enzymes, $2{,}125$ substrates, and $47{,}510$ usable samples ($4{,}362$ positive / $43{,}148$ negative), with substantially expanded plant P450 coverage. Second, a leakage-controlled external protocol identifies the 389 ESIBank-overlapping P450s via InterPro and removes matching enzymes from the test set. Third, a dual-graph architecture adds a residue-level Geometric Vector Perceptron (GVP) pathway to the original framework, with zero-initialization and delayed-unfreeze fusion preserving baseline equivalence at training step zero. On the $7{,}963$-sample filtered test set, the EZSpecificity checkpoint achieved AUC $= 0.5586$ and AUPR $= 0.1004$ (close to the $\approx 0.09$ random baseline at $1{:}10.2$ ratio), whereas our domain-specific baseline reached AUC $= 0.9205$ and AUPR $= 0.6403$ ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = +0.3619$). The dual-graph showed a marginal AUC gain of $+0.0008$ but AUPR dropped $-0.0275$ under single random split, with no significant benefit. Results suggest general-purpose state-of-the-art models exhibit a held-out enzyme generalization bottleneck on under-represented families, and domain-specific retraining substantially improves P450 specificity prediction.
\end{abstract*}
