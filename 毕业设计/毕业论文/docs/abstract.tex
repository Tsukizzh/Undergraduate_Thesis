\begin{abstract}
  酶底物特异性预测是生物催化、合成生物学与药物研发中的核心任务。2025 年发表于 \textit{Nature} 的 EZSpecificity 模型是该任务的代表性先进模型，论文声称其具有跨酶家族的通用预测能力。然而，其训练数据集 ESIBank 在细胞色素 P450 超家族上覆盖稀缺（25{,}225 条酶记录中仅 389 个 P450，占 $1.5\%$），而 P450 在药物代谢、植物次生代谢与农药降解中具有重要应用价值，可作为检验该通用性声明的严格外部评估基准。本研究开展三方面工作：其一，整合 RCSB PDB、ESIBank、P450Rdb、PlantP450DB 与 PCPD 五个来源构建 P450 专属基准数据集，跨源去重后含 1{,}622 个酶、2{,}125 个底物，最终可用样本 47{,}510 条（正 4{,}362 / 负 43{,}148），显著扩展植物 P450 覆盖；其二，以 InterPro 功能域识别 ESIBank 原始训练集中的 389 个 P450 并过滤测试集中对应的酶样本，建立泄漏控制的外部公平对比评估协议；其三，在原架构基础上并行引入残基级几何向量感知（GVP）通路构成双图架构，以零初始化加延迟解冻保证训练起步的基线等价性。在 7{,}963 条过滤后外部测试集上，EZSpecificity 公开预训练模型取得 Test AUC $= 0.5586$、AUPR $= 0.1004$（后者接近正负比 $1{:}10.2$ 的随机基线）；本研究领域专属基线模型达到 Test AUC $= 0.9205$、AUPR $= 0.6403$（$\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = +0.3619$）。双图架构在单随机切分评估下与基线模型表现相当（Test AUC 微幅提升 $+0.0008$、AUPR 回落 $-0.0275$），当前划分下未见显著收益。研究结果表明通用先进模型在训练集稀缺家族上存在明显的 未见酶的泛化瓶颈，领域专属数据重训可显著改善 P450 特异性预测表现。
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract*}
  Enzyme--substrate specificity prediction is a core task in biocatalysis, synthetic biology, and drug discovery. EZSpecificity, a cross-attention graph neural network published in \textit{Nature} in 2025, is a leading state-of-the-art method that claims generality across diverse enzyme families. However, its training dataset ESIBank substantially under-represents the cytochrome P450 superfamily: only 389 P450 enzymes ($1.5\%$ of ESIBank's 25{,}225 total enzymes) are included, whereas P450s hold significant importance in drug metabolism, plant secondary metabolism, and pesticide degradation---making the family a rigorous external benchmark for testing the model's generality claim. This thesis pursues three lines of work. First, a P450-specific benchmark dataset was constructed by integrating five complementary sources (RCSB PDB, ESIBank, P450Rdb, PlantP450DB, and PCPD), yielding $1{,}622$ unique enzymes, $2{,}125$ unique substrates, and $47{,}510$ usable samples ($4{,}362$ positive / $43{,}148$ negative after feature pipeline filtering), with substantially expanded coverage of plant P450s. Second, a leakage-controlled external evaluation protocol was established by strictly identifying the $389$ ESIBank-overlapping P450s through InterPro domain verification and filtering matching enzymes from the held-out test set. Third, a dual-graph architecture was designed on top of the original framework by introducing a residue-level Geometric Vector Perceptron (GVP) pathway, with a zero-initialization and delayed-unfreeze fusion design that preserves strict baseline equivalence at training step zero. Empirically, on the $7{,}963$-sample filtered test set, the public EZSpecificity checkpoint achieved Test AUC $= 0.5586$ and AUPR $= 0.1004$ (the latter close to the $\approx 0.09$ random baseline at $1{:}10.2$ class ratio), whereas our P450-specific baseline trained with identical architecture reached Test AUC $= 0.9205$ and AUPR $= 0.6403$ ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC} = +0.3619$). The dual-graph model showed a marginal Test AUC improvement of $+0.0008$ and a Test AUPR drop of $-0.0275$ under single random split evaluation, with no significant benefit observed in the current split. These results suggest that general-purpose state-of-the-art models exhibit a held-out enzyme generalization bottleneck on training-underrepresented enzyme families, and that domain-specific retraining substantially improves P450 substrate specificity prediction.
\end{abstract*}
